Illinois Sponsors operates the commercial business of sports and entertainment properties across a top-3 U.S. media market — under multi-year agreements that produce recurring, high-retention revenue.

The Thesis

A large category, consolidating — and a market it skipped.

Commercial rights for sports properties is an established, multi-billion-dollar category. National holding companies — Learfield, Playfly, JMI and others — have spent a decade consolidating college and pro representation into portfolios run from out of market.

That model leaves regional markets under-served: properties are bolted onto a national book, priced on rate cards, and sold by teams that don't live in the market. Illinois — a top-3 U.S. media market with a dozen Division I programs and eight major pro franchises — has no dedicated in-market operator running its commercial side.

Illinois Sponsors is building that operator: one company that holds the mandate, operates the full commercial business, and grows it from inside the market.

The Model

Recurring revenue, real retention.

  • Multi-year management agreements — revenue shares, guarantees and rights fees, not project work
  • High switching costs — once a property's commercial operation is rebuilt on our systems, it stays
  • Operating leverage — one sales, media and activation team across many properties
  • Aligned incentives — we're paid on the revenue we build, so growth compounds on renewal
Why Now

The tailwinds are lining up.

Schools are professionalizing commercial

NIL and budget pressure are pushing athletic departments to treat commercial rights as a real business, not an afterthought.

Conference realignment is resetting deals

Rights and sponsorship agreements are being renegotiated across the board — an opening for a new operator.

Streaming has fragmented media

Value is shifting to owned and operated inventory — exactly what an in-market operator can package and sell.

The nationals are over-extended

Portfolio-scale reps can't give a regional property real attention. Local depth is the wedge.
